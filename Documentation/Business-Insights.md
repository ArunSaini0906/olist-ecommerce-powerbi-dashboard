# Business Insights & Recommendations

## Executive Summary

The Olist E-Commerce Business Intelligence analysis evaluates commercial
performance, customer behavior, product and category performance,
geographic concentration, customer satisfaction, and delivery operations.

The analysis identified strong overall revenue generation and growth,
but also highlighted important opportunities around customer retention,
geographic concentration, product quality, and logistics performance.

> Note: Revenue figures represent merchandise revenue/GMV calculated from
> order-item revenue and should not be interpreted as net profit.

---

# 1. Revenue Performance

## Finding

The business generated approximately **R$13.59M** in merchandise revenue.

Revenue increased from approximately **R$6.16M in 2017** to approximately
**R$7.39M in 2018**, representing approximately **20% year-over-year growth**.

## Business Implication

The business demonstrated positive commercial growth during the analyzed
period.

The increase in revenue was accompanied by an increase in order volume,
indicating that growth was not solely driven by higher order values.

## Recommended Action

Continue investing in high-performing categories and markets while
identifying opportunities to increase customer purchase frequency and
average order value.

---

# 2. Geographic Revenue Concentration

## Finding

São Paulo generated approximately **R$5.20M**, representing approximately
**38.3% of total merchandise revenue**.

Rio de Janeiro generated approximately **R$1.82M**, representing approximately
**13.4% of total revenue**.

Together, São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro account for approximately
**51.7% of total merchandise revenue**.

## Business Implication

A significant proportion of business activity is concentrated in a small
number of geographic markets.

Strong performance in these markets is critical to overall business
performance, but high concentration also creates geographic dependency.

## Recommended Action

1. Protect service quality and product availability in São Paulo and Rio de
   Janeiro.
2. Maintain strong seller and logistics coverage in these markets.
3. Identify lower-penetration states with strong customer ratings and
   delivery performance.
4. Evaluate targeted expansion opportunities outside the two dominant
   markets.

---

# 3. Health & Beauty — Leading Revenue Category

## Finding

Health & Beauty is one of the strongest revenue-generating categories,
generating approximately **R$1.26M** in merchandise revenue.

Its contribution is approximately **9.3% of total merchandise revenue**.

## Business Implication

The category is commercially important and represents a significant source
of revenue.

## Recommended Action

Explore:

- Cross-selling opportunities
- Product bundles
- Repeat-purchase campaigns
- Personalized recommendations
- Seller expansion within the category

The category should be treated as a strategic growth area.

---

# 4. Watches & Gifts — Strong Revenue but Satisfaction Risk

## Finding

Watches & Gifts generates approximately **R$1.21M** in merchandise revenue,
representing approximately **8.9% of total revenue**.

However, its average customer rating is approximately **4.02**, slightly
below the overall average rating of approximately **4.09**.

## Business Implication

The category generates substantial revenue but has slightly weaker
customer satisfaction.

This creates a potential customer-experience risk in an economically
important category.

## Recommended Action

Investigate:

- Low-rated products
- Seller performance
- Product quality
- Product descriptions
- Delivery experience
- Customer complaints

The objective should be to protect revenue while improving customer
satisfaction.

---

# 5. Bed & Bath — Customer Experience Opportunity

## Finding

Bed & Bath generates approximately **R$1.04M** in merchandise revenue.

Its average rating is approximately **3.90**, below the overall rating of
approximately **4.09**.

## Business Implication

The category has meaningful commercial importance but weaker customer
satisfaction.

## Recommended Action

Identify the products and sellers responsible for low ratings.

Analyze whether negative reviews are associated with:

- Product quality
- Damaged products
- Incorrect products
- Seller issues
- Delivery delays
- Product descriptions

Prioritize high-revenue, low-rated products for corrective action.

---

# 6. Office Furniture — Major Product Quality Concern

## Finding

Office Furniture has an average rating of approximately **3.49**, which is
significantly below the overall average rating of approximately **4.09**.

## Business Implication

Customers appear substantially less satisfied with this category.

Although the category may not be among the largest revenue contributors,
poor customer experience can negatively affect repeat purchases,
reputation, and future category growth.

## Recommended Action

Perform SKU-level and seller-level investigation.

Prioritize:

1. Products with high sales and low ratings
2. Sellers with consistently poor ratings
3. Products with repeated negative reviews
4. Products associated with delivery problems

Consider improving product quality, seller standards, descriptions,
packaging, and fulfillment processes.

---

# 7. Customer Retention — Major Growth Opportunity

## Finding

The dataset contains approximately **96.1K unique customers**, while only
approximately **3.1%** are repeat customers.

Approximately **3K customers** made repeat purchases.

## Business Implication

The customer base is heavily weighted toward one-time purchasers.

This represents one of the largest potential commercial opportunities
identified in the analysis.

Increasing repeat purchasing can potentially improve customer value
without requiring the business to acquire an entirely new customer.

## Recommended Action

Implement a structured customer-retention strategy:

- Personalized product recommendations
- Post-purchase email campaigns
- Loyalty programs
- Category cross-selling
- Repeat-purchase incentives
- Personalized promotions
- Customer reactivation campaigns

Customer segmentation should be used to target high-value customers
differently from low-value customers.

---

# 8. Customer Value Is Highly Uneven

## Finding

Customer spending is highly distributed across value segments.

A small group of customers generates substantially more historical revenue
than the majority of customers.

The highest-value customer generated approximately **R$13.4K** in historical
merchandise spend, while the median customer generated approximately **R$89**.

## Business Implication

Treating every customer identically is unlikely to maximize customer
value.

High-value customers should receive different engagement strategies from
low-value or one-time customers.

## Recommended Action

Implement customer segmentation based on:

- Historical spend
- Order frequency
- Recency
- Category preferences
- Geographic location

Prioritize high-value and high-potential customers for retention and
personalized marketing.

---

# 9. Customer Satisfaction — Strong Average but Hidden Risk

## Finding

The overall average customer rating is approximately **4.09/5**.

Approximately **57.8%** of reviews are five-star reviews.

However, approximately **11.5%** of reviews are one-star reviews.

## Business Implication

The overall rating suggests generally positive customer satisfaction, but
the one-star review segment represents a meaningful group of dissatisfied
customers.

A strong average rating can therefore hide specific product, seller, or
delivery problems.

## Recommended Action

Analyze one- and two-star reviews by:

- Product category
- Product
- Seller
- State
- Delivery performance

Prioritize recurring negative patterns rather than focusing only on the
overall average rating.

---

# 10. Delivery Performance

## Finding

Approximately **91.9%** of delivered orders were delivered on time, while
approximately **8.1%** were delivered late.

There were approximately **96.5K delivered orders** in the analyzed data.

## Business Implication

Overall delivery performance is relatively strong, but approximately one
in every twelve delivered orders experienced a delay.

Late delivery can negatively affect customer satisfaction and repeat
purchasing.

## Recommended Action

Create an operational exception process focused on:

- High late-delivery states
- High late-delivery sellers
- High-risk categories
- Carrier performance
- Shipping routes
- Estimated delivery accuracy

---

# 11. Alagoas — Geographic Delivery Risk

## Finding

Alagoas recorded an estimated late-delivery rate of approximately **23.9%**
among delivered orders.

## Business Implication

The late-delivery rate is substantially higher than the overall late
delivery rate of approximately **8.1%**.

Although Alabama has lower absolute order volume than major markets, the
rate indicates a potential geographic logistics issue.

## Recommended Action

Investigate:

- Carrier coverage
- Seller locations
- Shipping routes
- Delivery distance
- Estimated delivery-date accuracy
- Logistics partner performance

Because the state has lower order volume, decisions should be based on both
late-delivery rate and order volume.

---

# 12. Bahia — Significant Delivery Risk

## Finding

Bahia recorded an estimated late-delivery rate of approximately **14.0%**.

## Business Implication

The state has a higher delivery-risk level than the overall business.

This may indicate geographic or logistics challenges that require further
investigation.

## Recommended Action

Analyze seller-to-customer shipping routes and carrier performance in Bahia.

Prioritize improvements where high delivery delays coincide with high
revenue or customer volume.

---

# 13. Freight Cost Is Commercially Significant

## Finding

Total freight cost is approximately **R$2.25M** compared with approximately
**R$13.59M** in merchandise revenue.

Freight therefore represents approximately **16.6% of merchandise revenue**.

## Business Implication

Shipping economics are significant enough to influence overall commercial
performance.

A high freight burden may reduce the economic attractiveness of lower-value
orders.

## Recommended Action

Analyze freight cost by:

- State
- Category
- Seller
- Order value
- Product size/weight where available

Identify categories and geographic markets where freight costs are
disproportionately high.

---

# 14. Revenue vs Customer Rating

## Finding

The Revenue vs Customer Rating analysis shows that high-revenue categories
are not necessarily the categories with the highest customer ratings.

Several commercially important categories have ratings below the overall
average.

## Business Implication

Revenue alone should not be used to determine which categories deserve
investment.

Management should evaluate both financial contribution and customer
experience.

## Recommended Action

Use a two-dimensional category framework:

| Revenue | Rating | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | Scale |
| High | Low | Fix |
| Low | High | Grow |
| Low | Low | Review |

This framework is implemented in the dashboard's Category Management
Action Matrix.

---

# 15. Overall Business Opportunity

The analysis suggests that the business has strong commercial potential,
but future growth should not rely only on acquiring more customers.

The largest opportunities are:

### 1. Increase Customer Retention

The approximately 3.1% repeat customer rate represents a significant
opportunity to increase customer lifetime value.

### 2. Protect Core Markets

São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro contribute approximately 51.7% of revenue and
should receive strong inventory, seller, and logistics support.

### 3. Improve Product Quality

Low-rated categories such as Office Furniture and Bed & Bath require
product- and seller-level investigation.

### 4. Improve Logistics

Late delivery rates above the overall average should be investigated by
state, seller, category, and carrier.

### 5. Optimize Freight Economics

Freight represents approximately 16.6% of merchandise revenue and should
be monitored alongside order value and geographic distance.

# Executive Action Framework

Based on the analysis, management actions can be grouped into four
priorities.

## RETAIN

**Objective:** Increase repeat purchases.

**Focus:**
- Loyalty
- Personalization
- Cross-selling
- Customer reactivation

**Reason:** Repeat customer rate is approximately 3.1%.

---

## GROW

**Objective:** Expand revenue while protecting strong markets.

**Focus:**
- High-performing categories
- Lower-penetration states
- Cross-selling
- Product expansion

**Reason:** São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro currently account for approximately
51.7% of revenue.

---

## FIX

**Objective:** Resolve customer experience and product quality problems.

**Focus:**
- Low-rated products
- Low-rated categories
- Seller performance
- Negative reviews

**Reason:** Some categories have materially lower ratings than the overall
4.09 average.

---

## OPTIMIZE

**Objective:** Improve logistics and shipping economics.

**Focus:**
- Late deliveries
- Carrier performance
- Shipping routes
- Freight costs
- High-risk states

**Reason:** Approximately 8.1% of delivered orders were late and freight
represents approximately 16.6% of merchandise revenue.

---

# Final Management Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the recommended priorities are:

1. **Build a customer-retention program** focused on converting one-time
   purchasers into repeat customers.

2. **Protect São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro**, which together represent more
   than half of merchandise revenue.

3. **Investigate low-rated categories**, especially Office Furniture and
   Bed & Bath.

4. **Create a delivery exception-management process** for states and sellers
   with significantly higher late-delivery rates.

5. **Optimize freight economics** by analyzing freight cost relative to
   order value.

6. **Use customer segmentation** to prioritize high-value customers for
   retention and personalized marketing.

7. **Monitor high-revenue, low-rating categories** because financial
   importance combined with poor customer experience represents a strategic
   risk.

8. **Evaluate lower-revenue but highly rated categories** as potential
   expansion opportunities.

# Important Analytical Notes

## Revenue

Revenue in this project represents merchandise revenue/GMV calculated from
order-item revenue.

It should not be interpreted as:

- Net profit
- Gross profit
- EBITDA
- Net sales after all expenses

## Delivery

Delivery KPIs are calculated using orders with the required delivery dates
available.

## Repeat Customers

A repeat customer is defined as a unique customer who placed more than one
order.

## Business Recommendations

Recommendations are based on observed relationships and patterns in the
dataset. They should be validated with additional business information
such as marketing costs, seller contracts, carrier performance data,
profit margins, inventory availability, and customer-acquisition costs
before operational implementation.
